Website / Mobile performance

PHONE TEST.Two views.

Lab test: 59/100 performance. Main-content appearance was 10.1 seconds; first visible content was 5.0 seconds.

Real mobile visitors: the latest 28-day data passes Google’s page-experience check. Main-content appearance was 2.3 seconds.

Transferred page weight: 1,658 KiB (about 1.6 MiB) in this mobile run. That is all transferred resources, not just images.

Practical fix: lighten the first screen, then retest both the slow-phone simulation and real-user results.

Scope: One simulated Moto G Power on slow 4G, October 30 at 10:06 a.m. PDT. LCP means the time until the largest visible content appears—not the time every page finishes loading.

Website / Desktop performance

DESKTOP.Keep it steady.

Lab test: 82/100, with main-content appearance at 1.9 seconds and a layout-movement score of 0.147.

Real desktop visitors: main content appeared in 1.6 seconds, but the page-experience check fails with layout movement at 0.12.

Transferred page weight: 1,710 KiB (about 1.7 MiB) in this desktop run.

Practical fix: reserve space for images and other late-loading items so content does not jump as people read.

Scope: CLS measures unexpected page movement; lower is better. Lab and real-user figures are different data sets, not contradictory measurements.

Website / First screen and order path

ORDERING.A working start.

The narrow-screen homepage has an ORDER button at the top. We did not find an absent first-screen order button.

Tapping ORDER, then Sacramento’s ORDER button, opened the Thanx menu with Midtown selected. That was two choices, with scrolling on the location page.

Keep this clear route. Use separate, clearly named links for an order today versus food for a group.

Scope: Checked in a roughly 400-pixel-wide resized browser, not on physical phone hardware. We opened the menu but did not add a cart item or complete checkout.

Website / Local orders and catering

TWO PATHS.Both are present.

The general order page has five store-specific links to Thanx. The catering page has five store-specific links to Toast.

The Sacramento catering link opened a real menu for the correct 2700 Capitol Ave address. This is not evidence of a broken catering route.

Keep the two purposes clear and test how order sources are measured before assuming different platforms are a problem.

Scope: Menus and location selection were checked. Delivery availability, payment and completed order tracking were not tested.

Website / Contact and catering inquiry

CONTACT.Finish the details.

Confirmed gap: the contact form has four optional store choices and omits Rocklin. The location page lists Rocklin.

The catering page offers an email route and a displayed phone number for planning help. A separate catering inquiry form was not shown on that page.

Practical fix: add Rocklin, confirm message routing, make the phone easy to tap and state a realistic reply window.

Scope: The optional store field does not prove the form cannot submit. We did not send a message, make a call or verify email delivery.

Website / Search basics

SEARCH BASICS.A useful cleanup.

Google’s public PageSpeed check flags a missing homepage meta description and images without alt text.

A meta description is a short page summary search engines may show. Alt text describes an image for people using screen readers.

Practical fix: write a clear homepage summary and useful descriptions for meaningful images. Do not promise a ranking change from those fixes alone.

Scope: This screenshot covers the tested homepage, not a full accessibility or search ranking audit.

Website / Public sitemap and placeholders

PUBLIC PAGES.Remove the leftovers.

The public page sitemap includes sample-page and t29-test-page. Both pages open publicly.

The sample page contains default WordPress example copy. The test page displays a test-page title.

Practical fix: remove unneeded placeholder pages and keep the sitemap focused on real customer pages.

Scope: A sitemap helps search engines discover pages. Inclusion is not proof a page is indexed or causing lost rankings.

Website / Measurement and crawl scope

MEASUREMENT.Check the handoff.

The PageSpeed resource list shows a Google Analytics 4 tag loaded from Google. That is evidence the tag loads—not that orders or leads are measured correctly.

Before running more ads, agree which actions matter: menu visits, order clicks, catering inquiries and qualified leads. Then check those actions in the owner’s tools.

The public sitemap and robots file are available. The robots file restricts the WordPress admin area, not the whole public site.

Scope: Private analytics reports, Meta event delivery, cross-platform order attribution and CRM follow-up were not verified. No claim of “no tracking” is made.

Google reviews / A small public sample

REVIEWS.A strong base.

Sacramento’s listing shows 4.5 stars from 506 reviews. The newest visible review in our sample was five days old.

That gives the team current customer language to learn from. Use themes—not copied testimonials without checking permissions—in future content.

Keep asking real customers for honest reviews and keep replies helpful. This review does not claim the business has unmanaged reviews.

Scope: A small Sacramento sample only, not a review-growth study or response-rate audit. Other branches’ review recency was not measured.

Social / Instagram and Facebook

SOCIAL.Activity to build on.

Instagram: 9,457 followers; the visible grid mixes short videos and still-image posts. The latest visible post was dated October 29, 2026.

Facebook: the page displayed about 10K followers and 94% recommend from 702 reviews. Its intro promotes catering and the business website.

Practical next step: turn the strongest food and catering stories into repeatable short videos, with one clear order or inquiry step.

Scope: One fresh Instagram post showed 25 likes and no comments. That is not enough to label overall engagement good or bad. Full posting frequency was not measured.

Local search / Sacramento-only snapshot

LOCAL SEARCH.A starting point.

We sampled “healthy lunch Sacramento,” “catering Sacramento” and “tea Sacramento.” TBar was not visible in the captured first result pages.

Competitors appeared for these broad choices. Use those searches to shape useful location and catering pages—not to promise a fixed Google position.

Next check: repeat by location, device and search area, then compare with the business’s actual Search Console and Google profile data.

Scope: Personalized, logged-in Sacramento-area snapshots. Not a fixed ranking report, not a map-grid study, and not a claim about all searchers or other cities.

Nearby examples / Learn, do not copy

LOCAL EXAMPLES.Make planning easy.

Mendocino Farms, Midtown: a dedicated location page puts local contact details, catering contact and order routes together.

Selland’s: its catering page separates office and gathering options and explains advance notice, delivery and how to arrange help.

Useful direction for TBar: answer the group planner’s first questions and give each location a clear, consistent path. These are examples—not proof either competitor converts better.

Scope: Public page observations only. No competitor results, ad spend or sales data were available.

Scope / Practical next step

WORK TOGETHER.Keep roles clear.

The plan supports the existing marketing and catering team. It does not assume an open role is unfilled or that TBar has no marketing owner.

Vessel: food and team content, approved local ad tests, useful location pages and consistent public details.

Sales lead: group-order relationships, qualified inquiries, follow-up and closing. Review what turns into useful conversations together.

  • Before changes: confirm correct hours, form routing and who owns each public profile.
  • Before ads: approve the budget and check measurement across the website, Thanx, Toast and the customer follow-up system.
  • After launch: review useful inquiries and orders, not just views or likes.

Scope: No private analytics, customer records or hiring system was accessed. No calls, emails, test inquiries, purchases or ad spend were made. A source crawl is not a full test of every page, form or external resource.
